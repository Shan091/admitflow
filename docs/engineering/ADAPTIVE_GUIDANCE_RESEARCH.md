# Adaptive course guidance: evidence and implementation

## Product decision

Guide this institution's applicants in English. Use an adaptive conversation with
optional deeper exploration; prioritize the student's fit over enrollment. A decided
applicant can proceed directly. A student who is comparing or uncertain gets context,
activity examples and a small, explainable shortlist. An absent offering must never
be replaced with an unrelated local course just to retain the inquiry.

This is **research-informed exploratory guidance**, not a validated psychological
assessment. No claim is made that the conversation predicts success, measures aptitude
or has been normed for Kerala students. Classification describes the support needed
now, rather than a permanent student type.

no assessment-access or counselor-availability claim depends on that redirect.

## Asking useful questions

A bounded interpretation step extracts explicit current-message evidence first.
Academic corrections are validated, catalog eligibility is refreshed, and then the
planner receives recent completed/sent conversation, accepted evidence, corrected
academic facts, the current tenant catalog and the current question. It chooses
the next question based on an unresolved decision, rather than walking a questionnaire.
Examples below illustrate intent, not a fixed script or copied test items:

| What is uncertain? | Useful exploration | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A familiar course name is the only option known | What activity or outcome attracts the student to it? Connect relevant less familiar catalog choices to that evidence. | Assuming the traditional course is wrong or repeatedly upselling. |
| Student says “I like computers” | Ask about something they tried: making a page, organizing information, repairing a device, creating media or another example. | Treating every computer interest as programming aptitude. |
| Family preference conflicts with the student's interests | Clarify the student's own priorities and the source of the disagreement. | Labelling the student indecisive or treating family wishes as their consent. |
| No experience with a field | Ask what seems interesting and suggest a small way to explore it. | Recording lack of exposure as dislike or inability. |
| Time, travel or budget matters | Invite an optional practical constraint and explain why it affects options. | Requesting household income, caste, diagnosis or unnecessary identifiers. |
| Applicant is decided | Answer relevant questions and permit explicit course choice. | Making a personality quiz compulsory. |
| Academic prerequisites are unknown | Ask only the relevant missing catalog-rule field; keep unknown separate from pass/fail. | Inferring marks, education or eligibility from interests. |

## Boundaries and verification

Structured OpenAI decisions interpret conversation; code controls catalog ownership,
open intakes, academic rules, source-backed course evidence and actual selections.
Each suggested course must reference a current course ID and quotations from both
the accepted student notes and catalog. External fields are labelled research leads,
not verified courses or providers. No generated tuition, salary, accreditation,
guaranteed admission or fit percentage is permitted.

The encrypted profile stores literal notes, tentative themes and the current support
stage. A new statement can replace a prior topic; unknowns and refusals remain visible.
This profile expires/erases with existing dialogue privacy controls. Staff see the
student's evidence and its limitations. No test results are imported from external sites.

Verification must include contextual variations, unfamiliar but relevant offerings,
clear preferences, conflicting interests, corrections, refusals, course unavailability,
cross-tenant IDs, provider failure, FAQ interruption, human/safety escalation and
explicit selections. Synthetic OpenAI scenarios can test these behaviors but do not
establish psychometric validity, language fluency or real-student outcomes.
